          How it works

          1. 01

            Clean

            The text is lowercased and stripped of links, HTML, numbers, punctuation and news-agency datelines. Common English stop words are ignored.

          2. 02

            Analyze

            TF-IDF weighs each word by how often it appears in the article and how distinctive it was across the training articles.

          3. 03

            Predict

            A logistic regression classifier combines the learned weights of those words to estimate whether the writing resembles articles labelled real or fake.

          Private by design

          Your article is analyzed locally in your browser. Your text is not uploaded to a server: the page only downloads the model once, then all analysis happens on your device.

          Fact-check search is optional and only runs when you click it. It sends just the short search phrase you choose, through this site's server, to Google's Fact Check Tools API.

          Important limitations

          This tool analyzes writing patterns, not factual accuracy. A high real score does not prove that an article is true, and a high fake score does not prove that it is false. Always verify important claims using trusted sources.

          • Trained on English-language political and world news from roughly 2015–2018; other topics, eras and languages are less reliable.
          • Short texts give unreliable results.
          • It can be fooled by fake stories written in a neutral, wire-service style, and vice versa.
